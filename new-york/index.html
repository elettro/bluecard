Holocaust Survivors in New York City

Direct financial assistance, emergency aid and community for Holocaust survivors in need across New York City.

New York survivors need our help

The Blue Card is a New York–based nonprofit and the only organization in the United States whose sole mission is to provide direct financial assistance to Holocaust survivors in need. “Many Holocaust survivors in New York City live alone, on limited incomes and without family nearby,” says Executive Director Masha Pearl. Our office is in Manhattan, and our programs reach survivor households across New York City. Survivors are referred by a social worker or agency. See how to refer a survivor.

Our support programs help New York survivors afford the essentials and live with dignity.

For social workers and agencies

Holocaust survivors seeking help must be referred by a social service agency or government agency. The Blue Card is an agency of last resort.

  1. Check eligibility. The survivor must have an urgent financial need, no other local or government agency able to help, and live in our service area under Claims Conference guidelines. Call 212-239-2251 or email info@bluecard.org to confirm the area.
  2. Complete the Referral Application. Make a clear recommendation about which program will help most, and include your agency address, phone (with extension) and email.
  3. Mail or email it. Do not fax. Mail to The Blue Card, Attn: The Blue Card Committee, 171 Madison Avenue, Suite 1405, New York, NY 10016, or email info@bluecard.org with the subject line “Application Form for The Blue Card Committee.”
  4. We review and respond. Every application is reviewed carefully and all information is kept confidential.

Questions about New York

How many Holocaust survivors live in New York City?

The Blue Card estimates that approximately 14,700 Holocaust survivors live in New York City, as of November 2025. Many live alone, on limited incomes and without family nearby.

What does The Blue Card do for survivors in New York City?

The Blue Card provides direct financial assistance, including emergency help, ongoing monthly support, medication and medical assistance, and health and well-being programs. It also organizes events, retreats and holiday deliveries for New York survivors.

How does a New York survivor apply for help?

A survivor must be referred by a social service agency or government agency. A case manager reviews the person’s income and expenses, then submits the Referral Application to The Blue Card by mail or email. Please do not fax applications.

Who is eligible?

To be eligible, a survivor must be referred by an agency, have an urgent financial need, have no other local or government agency that can help, and live within The Blue Card’s service area under Claims Conference guidelines. The Blue Card is an agency of last resort. Call 212-239-2251 or email info@bluecard.org to check whether a client lives in the service area.

Is applicant information kept confidential?

Yes. The Blue Card reviews each application carefully, and all information is kept confidential.

Where is The Blue Card located?

The Blue Card’s office is at 171 Madison Avenue, Suite 1405, New York, NY 10016. The phone number is 212-239-2251 and the email is info@bluecard.org.

How can I help Holocaust survivors in New York City?

You can donate, run with Team Blue Card in the TCS New York City Marathon or the NYC Half Marathon, volunteer, join the Young Leadership Committee, or bring a survivor speaker to your school or organization.

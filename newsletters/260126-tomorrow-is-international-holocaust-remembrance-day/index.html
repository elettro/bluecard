Dear Friends,

Tomorrow, January 27, the world will mark International Holocaust Remembrance Day.

This fall, at a Blue Card gathering, The Jewish Week interviewed Holocaust survivor Lillian Feintuch. In the video, Lillian shares pieces of her story in her own words, calmly and clearly, as someone who lived it.

She speaks about being just five and a half years old when two Nazis came to her door and ordered her family to pack. Her father was taken to slave labor. Her mother baked whatever flour she had left and made roux, food that would later save their lives during a week spent packed into cattle cars. She describes being forced into ghettos with no roof, marching while non Jewish neighbors watched from the sides, knowing they would return home to food and safety while she and her family were being taken away.

Lillian recounts standing near Auschwitz for two days, separated from her brothers in the chaos, and a moment when a Nazi pointed a gun at her forehead, threatening to kill her first so her mother would suffer. Somehow, miraculously, the trigger was never pulled. She speaks about her mother’s courage, about finding her brothers again, about narrowly surviving bombings, and about the kindness of a farmer who hid them and gave them bread and butter, a luxury she remembers decades later.

“I was a little girl,” Lillian says. “But I grew up overnight.”

International Holocaust Remembrance Day was established by the United Nations in 2005 so that stories like Lillian’s are not only remembered within the Jewish community, but heard by the world. It is a reminder of what happens when hatred is allowed to grow unchecked, and why memory carries responsibility.

For survivors, remembrance is not abstract. It is a lived experience. Childhoods were stolen. Families were torn apart. Lives were forever altered.

At The Blue Card, our mission is to ensure that survivors like Lillian never lose their dignity again. We help provide food, housing assistance, medical care, and essential support so Holocaust survivors can live their later years with security and respect.

If Lillian’s story moved you, I invite you to act. Your donation today helps ensure that Holocaust survivors receive the care and dignity they deserve, after everything they have already endured.

Remembrance must be paired with responsibility.

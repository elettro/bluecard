Dear Friends,

Because of you, Holocaust survivors across this country are living with dignity.

Through The Blue Card, we are supporting more than 3,000 survivor households with critical assistance, including food, medical care, housing, and more.

But I want to share something else that is just as important.

I was proud that our staff member, Georgi Goldman, was invited to join Bernie Furshpan, Vice Chair of the Holocaust Memorial and Tolerance Center of Nassau County and Executive Producer of Humanity Matters TV, on hmTv’s Ordinary Heroes, one of the most respected platforms for Holocaust education, in a conversation that makes the urgency of this moment clear.

That work is already happening on the ground: The Blue Card supports hundreds of Holocaust survivors across Long Island, including Nassau and Suffolk counties, while bringing our interactive hologram program to public, private, and Jewish schools throughout the region.

And as Georgi said in that conversation, “Over the next 10 to 15 years, 90% of Holocaust survivors will no longer be here. So how do we continue their legacy and make sure their voices are heard?”

That moment is already here.

Georgi is helping lead an effort to bring survivor stories directly into classrooms in a way that actually connects with students.

Students watch a survivor’s story, then they get to ask questions and hear answers in that survivor’s own voice. You can see the difference; they lean in, they ask real questions, and it stays with them.

As Georgi shared, “Teachers tell us the kids talk about it. They’re inspired. That kind of interaction is becoming more and more rare.”

As Passover begins tomorrow night, we are reminded of what it means to tell our story, pass it down, and ensure the next generation understands where we came from.

That is exactly what this work is about.

If this matters to you, I’m asking you to help us continue it.

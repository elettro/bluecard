Today is the anniversary of Kristallnacht,

“Night of Broken Glass”

Photo of the shattered stained glass windows of the Zerrennerstrasse Synagogue after its destruction on Kristallnacht, in Pforzheim, Germany.

Kristallnacht marks the start of Nazi officials discriminating and arresting hundreds of Jews for the sole reason that they were Jews, without any further cause for arrest. During those days of extreme violence by the German state, Jews in Germany and Austria were accosted in their dwellings by storm troopers, police and others. Many were beaten, rounded up or taken to concentration camps. Some were killed. Jewish-owned businesses were smashed and in many cases looted. Hundreds of synagogues were destroyed. Jewish schools, homes and graveyards vandalized.

As we remember Kristallnacht, we must also remember those who survived and are residing among us now. Most do not have enough financial stability to afford the most basin needs every person should have. With no family to fall back on, they look to The Blue Card, and you, for help.

The Blue Card cares and is there to offer its invaluable help to thousands of Holocaust survivors in need. Dignity was forcibly taken from them during the Holocaust, and The Blue Card ensures that its clients don't lose their dignity again.

The Blue Card is the only national organization in the United States whose sole mission is to help needy Holocaust survivors.

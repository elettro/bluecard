Today is the anniversary of Kristallnacht,

“Night of Broken Glass”

Synagogues razed, businesses vandalized, books burned, men kidnapped from their homes. Jews across Germany and German-occupied territories woke up to state-sponsored violence against them. 84 years ago today was the night of pogroms now known as Kristallnacht, or The Night of Broken Glass. Many count this as the beginning of the Holocaust.

On the night of November 9th, 1938 Inge Auerbacher was four years old but she still remembers hoodlums smashing every window in her family's house with rocks. She hid in the back shed with her mother and grandmother all night to escape the violence, but her father and grandfather were arrested with all the other Jewish men in the city and were taken to Dachau for weeks.

Kristallnacht was just the beginning.

As we remember Kristallnacht, we must also remember those who survived and are residing among us now. Most do not have enough financial stability to afford the most basin needs every person should have. With no family to fall back on, they look to The Blue Card, and you, for help.

The Blue Card cares and is there to offer its invaluable help to thousands of Holocaust survivors in need. Dignity was forcibly taken from them during the Holocaust, and The Blue Card ensures that its clients don't lose their dignity again.

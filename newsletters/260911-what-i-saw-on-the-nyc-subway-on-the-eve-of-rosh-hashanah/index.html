What I Saw on the NYC Subway on the Eve of Rosh Hashanah

← Back to Newsletters September 11, 2026

From Masha Pearl. Originally sent to The Blue Card email community on September 11, 2026.

Dear Friends,

Last night, I walked onto the 1 train and was shocked at what I saw:

A swastika carved into a seat.

I locked eyes with a frail elderly woman and quickly sat down to cover the swastika.

The passengers around me seemed either unaware of it or unbothered by it. That troubled me, too. Today, as we remember those we lost on September 11 and prepare to welcome Rosh Hashanah, that image has stayed with me.

I reported what I saw and asked that it be addressed. I am grateful to Governor Hochul’s office for responding quickly and contacting the MTA to have it removed.

The survivors we serve at The Blue Card know exactly what that symbol means. They lived through the hatred it represents. They should be able to ride the subway without being confronted by it.

I don’t know who carved it or how much they understood about its history. But children need to learn what a swastika means, what happened under that flag, and why seeing it can be so painful for survivors and their families. With the school year just beginning, this deserves attention in our classrooms.

The Blue Card is committed to confronting hate symbols and hatred in all its forms. Through our Holocaust education work, we will help students understand the history and harm of these symbols and why speaking out against hate matters.

At The Blue Card, caring for survivors remains our first responsibility. We help pay for food, rent and medical care, and we will continue doing so for as long as they need us. We are also expanding our Holocaust education work so more children can learn directly from survivors’ experiences.

Our interactive program featuring Sonia Warshawski allows students to ask questions and hear her recorded testimony. We want to bring it into more schools, develop additional survivor holograms, and give teachers practical resources to address antisemitism, Holocaust denial and hate symbols.

You can help us reach those classrooms. If you know a teacher, principal or school leader who would welcome this program, please introduce us or contact info@bluecard.org. We would love to work with them.

As we enter Rosh Hashanah, please also consider making a gift to help us care for survivors who are struggling to afford food, rent and medical bills. Your generosity allows us to keep showing up for them when they need us.

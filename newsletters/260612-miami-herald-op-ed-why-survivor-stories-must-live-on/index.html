Dear Friends,

I wanted to share my new op ed in the Miami Herald about a question I hear often from Holocaust survivors: Will my story still matter when I am gone?

It is a heartbreaking question, and one that feels especially urgent right now. As survivors age, we are in a critical moment. We must do everything we can to preserve their stories, but we must do it carefully, honestly, and with deep respect for the words they chose to share.

In the piece, I write about the promise and responsibility of using AI in Holocaust education. Technology can help more people hear directly from survivors, including students who may never have the chance to meet a survivor in person. But it can never replace the truth, dignity, and humanity of their testimony.

At The Blue Card, this work is personal. Every day, we support Holocaust survivors who are still living with the trauma and hardships of what they endured. We also carry the responsibility of making sure their voices are heard by future generations.

I hope you will take a moment to read the op ed in the Miami Herald.

Read the op ed here.

As the last generation of Holocaust survivors continues to age, our obligation is clear. We must care for them now, while also making sure their memories are not lost, distorted, or reduced to history lessons without human voices behind them. Their stories are not only about the past. They are a warning, a responsibility, and a legacy we must protect together.

Your support makes this work possible. It helps us care for survivors today while also protecting their stories for tomorrow. Please consider making a gift to The Blue Card so we can continue standing with survivors with the urgency, dignity, and compassion they deserve.

With gratitude,

Masha Pearl

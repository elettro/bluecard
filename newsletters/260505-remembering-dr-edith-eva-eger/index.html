Dear Friends,

We are deeply saddened by the passing of Dr. Edith Eva Eger, a Holocaust survivor, psychologist, author, speaker, mother, and extraordinary voice of courage and healing.

Dr. Eger survived Auschwitz and went on to dedicate her life to helping others confront pain, find strength, and choose hope. Through her books, her speaking, and her work as a psychologist, she reached countless people with a message that was both deeply personal and profoundly universal.

The Blue Card had the great honor of speaking with Dr. Eger in 2022 for our podcast, together with her daughter. It was a conversation we will never forget. She spoke with honesty, grace, and strength about her survival, her memories, and the responsibility we all share to remember.

Her memoir, The Choice, helped bring the lessons of her life to readers around the world. But Dr. Eger was more than her remarkable story. She was a woman of warmth, wisdom, resilience, and light. She reminded us that even after unimaginable darkness, the human spirit can still choose dignity, love, and life.

At The Blue Card, we are privileged every day to serve Holocaust survivors who, like Dr. Eger, carry histories that must never be forgotten. Her passing is a painful reminder of how urgent this work remains. We must continue to listen, care, support, and honor survivors while they are still with us.

As we remember Dr. Eger’s life and legacy, we are reminded of the responsibility we have to care for the Holocaust survivors still with us today. In her memory, please consider making a gift to The Blue Card to help provide survivors in need with food, emergency assistance, medical support, home care, and the dignity they deserve.

May Dr. Edith Eva Eger’s memory be a blessing. May her family be comforted, and may her legacy continue to inspire all of us.

With sadness and respect,

Masha Pearl

Executive Director

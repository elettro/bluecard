Holocaust Survivors Served by The Blue Card Address Antisemitism During White House Visit

← Back to Newsletters July 22, 2026

From Masha Pearl. Originally sent to The Blue Card email community on July 22, 2026.

Support Holocaust Survivors

Dear Friend,

Last week, five extraordinary Holocaust survivors were welcomed to the White House for a personal meeting with President Trump in the Oval Office.

Mrs. Blau, Mrs. Cohen, Mrs. Rabinowitz, Mrs. Wohlberg and Mrs. Lee have each been part of The Blue Card family. We have had the privilege of supporting them through difficult moments, welcoming them to our programs and events and developing meaningful relationships with them over many years.

That is why seeing these remarkable women standing proudly in the Oval Office was so deeply personal and moving for all of us at The Blue Card.

During the meeting, President Trump listened as the survivors spoke about what they endured during the Holocaust, how they rebuilt their lives and why their stories must continue to be shared with future generations.

At a time when antisemitism is once again on the rise, there could be no more powerful reminder than Holocaust survivors sharing their experiences directly with the President of the United States.

The Blue Card was proud to partner in helping make this extraordinary visit possible and to see these women welcomed with the dignity, warmth and respect they deserve.

I am also sharing a handwritten letter below that Mrs. Rabinowitz previously sent to The Blue Card. In it, she described surviving Auschwitz, the gas chambers and a death march alongside her sister and thousands of other Jewish women.

She also wrote poignantly that “fewer and fewer of us are still alive because of our age” and expressed her determination to continue sharing her story.

At the conclusion of her letter, Mrs. Rabinowitz wrote:

“I am very grateful to Blue Card for all the financial and other help you give to us Holocaust survivors.”

Her words beautifully reflect the relationships The Blue Card has been privileged to build with survivors over many decades.

The survivors we serve are not simply names on an application or individuals receiving assistance. They are people we know, people we care about and cherished members of The Blue Card family.

We are there when a survivor needs help paying for food, housing, medical care or an unexpected emergency. We are there when someone feels alone or forgotten. We are also committed to ensuring that survivors have opportunities to share their stories and receive the recognition they so richly deserve.

After everything Mrs. Rabinowitz and the other survivors endured, it must have been extraordinary for them to enter the Oval Office, speak directly with the President and know that their voices were being heard at the highest level of our government.

It was an unforgettable experience for them and a deeply proud moment for The Blue Card.

But our work continues every day.

As the survivor population grows older, their needs are becoming more urgent. Your support allows us to continue providing compassionate, direct assistance and to remind survivors that they have not been forgotten.

Please consider making a gift today in honor of Mrs. Blau, Mrs. Cohen, Mrs. Rabinowitz, Mrs. Wohlberg, Mrs. Lee and all the Holocaust survivors who depend upon The Blue Card.

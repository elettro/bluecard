A Holocaust survivor shares her story and preaches kindness.

Goldie Jacoby. PALM SPRINGS, CA

Goldie, an 85-year-old survivor of the Holocaust, witnessed horrible atrocities early in life. When the Germans invaded Poland, she and her family went begging for food and ran into the forest to hide. Eventually they were taken in by a farmer who hid 10 people in a small pigsty.

They slept on one another's shoulders and were nearly eaten alive by lice. "Every single step for three years was a nightmare, trauma I will never forget," she says.

Years later, Goldie was married, a young mother of two, and suicidal. But at some point, she says, a lightbulb went on for her. "I knew I needed to make a decision. Do I want to live?

Or do want to die?"

While she could never excuse the perpetrators for what she and the Jewish community endured, Goldie recognized that dwelling in the past only stole joy from the present. She vowed to move forward and to stand for kindness

"When you forgive something, it's for your own self," she says. "You just have to go on with your life and make it better."

Goldie and her late husband, a fellow survivor who lost his family in concentration camps, dedicated their lives to sharing their harrowing ordeal with younger generations, speaking at schools and events hosted by The Blue Card, a nonprofit that offers financial assistance to Holocaust survivors.

Teachers often tell Goldie that after her visits students are kinder and more respectful to one another.

"I speak up because you cannot allow hatred to keep festering." she says

"My forgiveness is through trying to make this a better world!

Support Survivors on Yom HaShoah

Dear Friends,

Tonight, as the sun sets, we enter Yom HaShoah, Holocaust Remembrance Day. In Jewish tradition, a new day begins at sundown, and so this sacred time of memory and reflection begins now.

We remember the six million Jews who were murdered in the Holocaust. We remember entire families, entire communities, entire worlds that were destroyed.

And we think about the people who are still here.

People like Ruth.

Ruth is 92. She lives alone in a small apartment in Queens. When we first met her, she said something I have not been able to shake:

“I survived everything. I just didn’t think I would end up like this.”

She was a little girl in Hungary when her life was torn apart. She lost her parents. She survived.

When we met her, she was stretching meals to afford medication. Some days she ate very little. Some nights, she told us, it just felt very quiet.

A staff member sat with her, not rushing, just listening. And slowly, she started to talk. About her childhood. About what she lost. About what she remembers.

That moment mattered.

And moments like that matter even more right now.

Because we are seeing things in this country that should make all of us uncomfortable. Things that many of these survivors recognize in ways the rest of us never will.

That is part of what today is about.

Not just remembering the past, but showing up for the people who are still carrying it.

At The Blue Card, we support more than 3,000 Holocaust survivor households across the country. We help with food, medical care, home support, and the basics that no one should have to worry about at this stage of life.

We also make sure someone is there. To check in. To listen. To remind them they are not alone.

Today, I am asking you to be part of that.

To do something real, on a day that should never feel routine.

On Yom HaShoah, we ask you to join the 1,022 monthly donors by clicking here.

Your support helps survivors like Ruth live with dignity, and reminds them that they are still seen, still valued, and not forgotten.

Thank you for being part of this,

Masha Pearl

Executive Director
